## How this works

### Readings

The textbook is **Think Java** (Downey & Mayfield). It is **free**, it is online, and nothing
tracks whether you read it — it is a reference you look things up in, not a graded activity.

- Read online: <https://books.trinket.io/thinkjava2/>
- PDF, HTML, and code: <https://greenteapress.com/wp/think-java-2e/>

> **Do not buy a zyBooks code.** If the bookstore emailed you one, ignore it.

Five weeks list **no reading**. That is not an oversight — the book has no chapter on enums,
state machines, exceptions, or `Map`. Those weeks are carried by lecture and by the lab.

### Labs and drills

**Skill Builders** (SB1–SB7) are the main labs. You are graded by the tests already in the
repo, so you always know where you stand before you submit.

**Drills** are small — roughly 10–20 minutes each — and auto-graded.

**Recitation repos** are started with me in section and **finished at home.** Section is
fifty minutes and most of them hold more than fifty minutes of work — that is deliberate,
not a miscalculation. What matters in the room is that you leave with it cloned, running,
and pushed; the rest is yours to finish by Sunday. They count toward the **Recitation**
10%, and like everything else they are graded from the pull request.

Everything is handed in by **pushing to GitHub**. Committing saves your work on your laptop;
only pushing makes it exist for me. *Committed is not submitted.*

### Quizzes

**7 quizzes**, 5–8 questions, **10 minutes at the start of class**. The day is announced the
class before.

On paper. **No notes, no AI.** They test reading Java and predicting what it does — the
things that trip up Python programmers specifically (`5/2` is `2`, `'A' + 1` is `66`).

**Your lowest two are dropped.**
